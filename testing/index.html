Quick answer

Where can you see spectacled bears in Ecuador?

For Mindo Bird Watching's private experiences, the guide evaluates Antisana and Cayambe-Coca close to the tour date. The final route depends on recent activity, access, weather, visibility and your pickup plan. No ethical operator can guarantee a wild bear sighting.

Same species

Andean bear and spectacled bear are two names for Tremarctos ornatus.

Mountain habitat

The species uses cloud forest, montane forest and adjoining páramo landscapes.

Mostly plant-based

Fruits, bromeliads, palms and other vegetation form much of its diet.

Conservation status

The species is classified as Vulnerable and depends on connected Andean habitat.

Natural history

Meet the Andean bear

A few natural-history details explain why these bears can be difficult to find and why ethical observation matters.

01

Strong climbers

Andean bears can climb trees and may use platforms for feeding or resting. Forest structure, food availability and connected habitat strongly influence where they move.

Unique facial markings

Cream-colored markings vary by individual and may look like partial or complete spectacles.

Usually solitary

Adults are generally encountered alone, except for mothers with cubs or temporary food concentrations.

No hibernation

Movement is shaped more by food, weather, reproduction and disturbance than by winter dormancy.

Responsible viewing is part of the experience

A good wildlife day is measured by responsible decisions, not by forcing a sighting.

✓

Keep distance

Observe through optics. Never approach, surround or block a travel route.

✓

No feeding or baiting

✓

✓

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Are Andean bears and spectacled bears the same?

Yes. Both names refer to Tremarctos ornatus, the only living bear native to South America.

Are sightings guaranteed?

No. A guide can improve route selection and observation strategy, but wild bears cannot be guaranteed.

Is Antisana or Cayambe-Coca better?

There is no permanent winner. Current activity, visibility, access, weather and logistics determine the stronger route.

Can I combine the experience with Mindo birding?

Yes. A two-day birding and bear itinerary is one of the clearest ways to connect Mindo with high-Andean wildlife.

What should I bring?

Warm layers, rain protection, sun protection, comfortable footwear, water and a camera. Binoculars and a spotting scope are provided.

Is a full day better than a half day?

A full day provides more time to wait and adjust. The extended half day works well for tighter itineraries.
